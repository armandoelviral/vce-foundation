# FI-002 — What Differentiates Knowledge from Memory?

Status

Foundational Investigation

---

## Research Question

What differentiates knowledge from memory?

---

## Motivation

During the evolution of the Scientific Product
Program, the laboratory repeatedly observed that
conversations, notes and recollections were not
equivalent to institutional knowledge.

The creation of RESEARCH-001 marked a transition
from conversational memory toward explicit,
version-controlled institutional memory.

This observation raised a broader question.

Does memory become knowledge simply by being
stored?

Or are additional properties required?

---

## Working Questions

Can memory exist without knowledge?

Can knowledge exist without institutional memory?

What transforms information into knowledge?

What transforms knowledge into organizational
capability?

---

## Initial Observations

Current evidence suggests that memory and
knowledge are related but distinct.

Memory appears to preserve.

Knowledge appears to explain.

Memory stores.

Knowledge predicts.

Memory records.

Knowledge enables decisions.

These observations remain provisional.

---

## Research Objectives

Investigate:

• properties unique to memory;

• properties unique to knowledge;

• role of reproducibility;

• role of evidence;

• role of organizational learning;

• relationship with Scientific Products.

---

## Success Criteria

The investigation will be considered mature
when:

- memory and knowledge can be operationally
  distinguished;

- measurable criteria exist for both;

- Scientific Products can explicitly
  institutionalize each.

## Laboratory Note

A recurring question emerged during the
laboratory:

> Institutional memory preserves what was learned.

> Knowledge explains why it should be learned.

Whether this distinction survives future
investigation remains an open scientific
question.
