# FI-003 — What Differentiates Knowledge from Capability?

Status

Foundational Investigation

---

## Research Question

What differentiates knowledge from capability?

---

## Motivation

Throughout the Scientific Product Program,
knowledge repeatedly appeared insufficient
to explain organizational improvement.

Organizations frequently possess knowledge
that never becomes operational capability.

Conversely, mature capabilities appear to
depend upon knowledge but also require
institutionalization, reproducibility,
governance and execution.

This distinction motivates the present
investigation.

---

## Working Questions

Can knowledge exist without capability?

Can capability exist without explicit knowledge?

When does knowledge become operational?

What transforms individual knowledge into
institutional capability?

---

## Initial Observations

Current evidence suggests that knowledge
and capability represent different levels
of organizational maturity.

Knowledge explains.

Capability performs.

Knowledge understands.

Capability repeatedly produces outcomes.

Knowledge belongs to people.

Capability belongs to systems.

These observations remain provisional.

---

## Research Objectives

Investigate:

• operational characteristics of capability;

• relationship between knowledge and execution;

• role of institutionalization;

• role of reproducibility;

• relationship with Scientific Products;

• relationship with Verifiable Cognitive Infrastructure.

---

## Success Criteria

The investigation will be considered mature
when:

- operational criteria distinguish knowledge
  from capability;

- Scientific Products can explicitly model
  both concepts;

- capability evolution becomes measurable.
