# Purpose

Define the foundational concepts governing Program Capabilities and Institutional Capabilities within the VCE Research Program.

Capabilities represent the permanent outcome produced through the governed integration of human expertise, operational evidence, knowledge and methodology.

---

# Program Capability

## Definition

A Program Capability is a permanent ability acquired by the VCE Research Program through governed research, validated evidence and institutionalized knowledge.

Program Capabilities improve the Research Program itself.

Examples include:

- Research Freeze
- Methodology Versioning
- Legacy Integration
- Scientific Product Factory
- Knowledge Promotion Governance

Program Capabilities remain available for all future Research Cycles.

---

# Institutional Capability

## Definition

An Institutional Capability is a permanent organizational ability created through the governed integration of:

- Human Expertise
- Operational Evidence
- Institutional Knowledge
- Artificial Intelligence
- Organizational Governance

Institutional Capabilities increase the long-term effectiveness of an organization independently of any individual contributor.

Institutional Capabilities constitute the primary outcome of Scientific Products.

---

# Institutional Capability Principle

Organizations do not become permanently stronger by accumulating software.

Organizations become permanently stronger by continuously transforming validated human expertise into governed institutional capabilities.

Scientific Products exist to enable that transformation.

---

# Capability over Automation Principle

Automation is a mechanism.

Institutional Capability is the objective.

Automation shall never be considered a success criterion unless it contributes to measurable growth of organizational capability.

---

# Human Expertise Amplification Principle

Scientific Products shall preserve, amplify and institutionalize human expertise rather than replace it.

Every meaningful interaction with domain experts represents an opportunity to increase the future capability of the organization.

Expert knowledge is not consumed.

It is transformed into institutional capability.

---

# Evidence Supremacy Principle

Evidence has priority over architecture.

Architecture exists to explain evidence.

Architecture shall never be modified to protect assumptions from reproducible evidence.

Whenever conflict exists between architecture and validated evidence, architectural evolution shall be preferred.

---

# Institutional Capability Growth (ICG)

## Definition

Institutional Capability Growth measures the rate at which an organization creates, validates, adopts and reuses institutional capabilities.

ICG evaluates organizational evolution rather than software performance.

Possible indicators include:

- Reuse of validated knowledge
- Reduction in onboarding time
- Increase in decision consistency
- Reduction of repeated errors
- Growth of governed organizational policies
- Improvement in decision quality over time

The objective is continuous capability growth.

---

# Automation Bias Risk

## Definition

Automation Bias occurs when optimization of automation reduces long-term institutional capability.

Examples include:

- replacing expert reasoning without preserving it;
- optimizing efficiency while losing organizational knowledge;
- increasing automation but decreasing learning;
- removing human judgment from decisions requiring contextual expertise.

Scientific Products shall actively monitor and mitigate Automation Bias.

# Closing Statement

The ultimate objective of the VCE Research Program is not software production.

It is the governed creation of permanent capabilities.

Scientific Products are the operational mechanism through which organizations continuously transform human expertise, operational evidence and institutional knowledge into measurable institutional capability.

Technology enables this process.

People initiate it.

Evidence validates it.

Governance preserves it.

Capability is its permanent outcome.
