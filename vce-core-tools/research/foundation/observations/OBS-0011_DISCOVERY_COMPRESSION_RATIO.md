# OBS-0011 — Discovery Compression Ratio

Version

1.0

Status

Laboratory Observation

---

## Observation

For approximately every twenty units of discovery,
the laboratory should dedicate approximately eighty
units of effort to:

- compression;

- validation;

- implementation;

- redundancy elimination;

- architectural simplification.

---

## Interpretation

Discovery expands the set of possibilities.

Compression transforms those possibilities into
coherent and operational understanding.

The ratio is not intended as an exact numerical law.

It is an operational heuristic intended to prevent
conceptual expansion from exceeding the laboratory's
capacity to validate and institutionalize its discoveries.

---

## Motivation

Uncompressed discovery increases:

- conceptual entropy;

- architectural fragmentation;

- scientific debt;

- duplicated terminology;

- unsupported theory.

Compression increases:

- explanatory density;

- implementation evidence;

- architectural coherence;

- institutional memory;

- falsifiability.

---

## Operational Consequence

After a discovery-intensive phase,
the laboratory should prioritize:

1. implementation;

2. negative testing;

3. validation;

4. consolidation;

5. removal of redundant concepts;

6. stabilization of public contracts.

New theoretical expansion should remain constrained
until the previous discovery cycle has produced
sufficient operational evidence.

---

## Current Application

This observation currently governs the HAS engineering
program through an approximate allocation of:

- twenty percent discovery;

- eighty percent implementation, validation,
  observability and invariant protection.

---

## Epistemic Status

This is a laboratory observation.

It is not a scientific law or institutional principle.

Its validity must continue to be evaluated through
future engineering and research cycles.
