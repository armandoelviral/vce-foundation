# Comparative Analysis of Multi-Agent Systems, Agent-Based Modeling, Organizational Modeling and Verifiable Organizational Algebra

## Purpose

This review evaluates whether the proposed concept of a Verifiable Organizational Algebra is genuinely distinct from existing work in:

- Multi-Agent Systems
- Agent-Based Modeling
- Organizational Modeling
- Workflow / BPM
- Provenance-aware systems

The objective is not to defend novelty.

The objective is to determine whether the proposed model adds a distinct research contribution or merely reformulates existing approaches.

---

# Research Question

Does Verifiable Organizational Algebra introduce a distinct object of study, or is it already covered by existing theories of agents, organizations, workflows or simulations?

---

# Candidate Hypothesis

## DH-012 — Verifiable Organizational Algebra

A Verifiable Organizational Algebra models organizations not primarily as collections of actors, workflows or populations, but as collections of admissible role transitions whose legitimacy can be reconstructed, audited and formally evaluated.

# Preliminary Distinction

## Multi-Agent Systems

Focus:

Agent autonomy, interaction, communication, negotiation, cooperation and competition.

Typical structure:

State

↓

Decision

↓

Action

↓

New State

Potential gap:

State legitimacy is often assumed rather than reconstructed as a first-class object.

---

## Agent-Based Modeling

Focus:

Emergent behavior produced by populations of interacting agents.

Typical structure:

Population

↓

Local interaction

↓

Emergent pattern

Potential gap:

The primary concern is emergence, not necessarily reconstructable legitimacy of every transition.

---

## Organizational Modeling

Focus:

Roles, responsibilities, goals, workflows and organizational structure.

Examples to review:

- i*
- Tropos
- Gaia
- OperA
- MOISE+

Potential gap:

These approaches model organizations, but may not require every role transition to be governed by verifiable evidence, replayability and admissibility rules.

---

# Candidate Differentiator

In Verifiable Organizational Algebra, verifiability is not an audit layer added after execution.

It is a structural property of every admissible transition.

Candidate structure:

Intent

↓

Role Transition

↓

Validate

↓

Evaluate

↓

Acknowledge

↓

Logical Commit

↓

Physical Commit

↓

Observe

↓

Replay

---

# Falsification Question

Are there classes of organizations for which a representation based on verifiable roles and AVST-governed transitions is insufficient to explain, reconstruct or govern operational behavior?

If yes, DH-012 must be revised.
