# Methodological Self-Critique

## Purpose

The objective of this document is to identify weaknesses in the research methodology itself before they become structural weaknesses of the program.

The purpose is not to defend VCE.

The purpose is to improve VCE.

The program assumes that every research methodology contains blind spots.

Those blind spots must become explicit objects of investigation.

Failure to identify methodological weaknesses shall never be interpreted as evidence that none exist.

---

# Fundamental Question

What could make this research program systematically wrong?

---

# Methodological Risks

## 1. Confirmation Bias

Risk:

Researchers may preferentially seek evidence supporting their own hypotheses.

Potential consequence:

False confidence.

Mitigation:

- Mandatory Falsification Gate.
- Explicit search for contradictory evidence.
- Preservation of negative results.

---

## 2. Novelty Bias

Risk:

The program may overestimate the originality of its ideas.

Potential consequence:

Reinvention of existing theories.

Mitigation:

- Mandatory literature review.
- Cross-disciplinary comparison.
- Explicit novelty assessment.

---

## 3. Authority Bias

Risk:

Ideas may gain acceptance because of their authors rather than because of evidence.

Potential consequence:

Dogmatization.

Mitigation:

- Anonymous review when possible.
- Provenance reconstruction.
- Evidence over authorship.

---

## 4. Goodhart Epistemological Risk

Risk:

Researchers optimize epistemic metrics instead of improving knowledge.

Examples:

- producing documentation instead of understanding;
- maximizing traceability instead of explanatory power;
- increasing evidence quantity while decreasing evidence quality.

Potential consequence:

Methodological bureaucracy.

Mitigation:

Evidence quality shall always take precedence over metric optimization.

---

## 5. Product Capture

Risk:

Commercial success begins to dictate scientific conclusions.

Potential consequence:

The research agenda becomes market-driven.

Mitigation:

Operational evidence shall inform hypotheses but shall never replace scientific evaluation.

---

## 6. Terminology Inflation

Risk:

Renaming existing concepts creates an illusion of novelty.

Mitigation:

Every major concept shall include a bibliographic comparison before originality is claimed.

---

## 7. Survivorship Bias

Risk:

Only successful hypotheses remain visible.

Potential consequence:

Loss of valuable negative knowledge.

Mitigation:

Rejected hypotheses, failed experiments and counterexamples shall remain part of the Research Ledger.

---

## 8. Constitutional Capture

Risk:

Constitutional rules become unquestionable.

Potential consequence:

Loss of scientific adaptability.

Mitigation:

Every Constitutional Decision shall preserve reconstructable provenance.

No constitutional artifact shall be permanently immune from review.

---

## 9. Historical Rewrite

Risk:

Past decisions are retrospectively modified to match current understanding.

Potential consequence:

Loss of research continuity.

Mitigation:

Research Ledger shall remain append-only.

Every revision shall preserve its historical context.

---

## 10. Complexity Drift

Risk:

The methodology becomes so complex that researchers stop following it.

Potential consequence:

The program becomes self-defeating.

Mitigation:

The methodological cost of every new rule shall be explicitly evaluated.

Complexity must justify itself through measurable research value.

---

# Guiding Principle

Methodological rigor shall never become an end in itself.

Its purpose is to improve the quality, transparency and reconstructability of scientific reasoning.

Whenever methodology ceases to improve research, the methodology itself becomes a legitimate object of criticism.

---

# Evaluation Criterion

The quality of this program shall not be measured by:

- the number of hypotheses;
- the number of publications;
- the number of artifacts;
- the number of documents.

It shall be measured by its demonstrated ability to:

- discover its own errors;
- explain why corrections occurred;
- preserve the reasoning behind those corrections;
- improve its criteria through evidence.

---

# Closing Reflection

A mature research program is not the one that preserves the largest number of ideas.

A mature research program is the one that remains permanently worthy of being corrected.

The objective of VCE is therefore not to protect conclusions.

It is to protect the process through which conclusions earn the right to remain.
