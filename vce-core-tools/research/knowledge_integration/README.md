# Knowledge Integration Architecture (KIA)

## Purpose

The Knowledge Integration Architecture governs how external scientific knowledge becomes part of the internal VCE Research Program.

The architecture explicitly separates observation from adoption.

External scientific work is never incorporated directly into the research corpus.

Instead, it progresses through explicit stages of normalization, comparison, evaluation and review.

This separation preserves methodological transparency and reconstructability.

---

# Two Knowledge Domains

The VCE Research Program distinguishes between two complementary domains.

## External Scientific Knowledge

Knowledge produced outside the research program.

Examples:

- journal articles
- standards
- books
- technical reports
- reference implementations

This knowledge is represented through Evidence Cards.

---

## Internal Scientific Knowledge

Knowledge generated by the VCE Research Program.

Examples:

- Design Hypotheses
- Scientific Hypotheses
- Models
- Formalizations
- Evidence
- Accepted Principles

This knowledge is represented through the Research Registry.

---

# Integration Principle

External knowledge never modifies internal knowledge directly.

It first becomes:

Evidence Card

↓

Comparative Evidence

↓

Research Review

↓

Knowledge Integration

↓

Registry Update

---

# Guiding Principle

The VCE Research Program preserves explicit provenance between:

what the scientific community has published,

and

what the research program has decided to adopt.

The distinction is intentional.

Scientific integrity depends on preserving this separation.

---

# Long-Term Objective

Future researchers should be able to reconstruct not only the knowledge adopted by VCE, but also the complete chain of external evidence that justified every adoption.

Knowledge integration is therefore treated as a first-class scientific process.
