# Constitutional Principle

Identifier

CP-001

Title

Cross-Domain Normative Independence

Version

0.2

Status

Draft

Model

Reduced Constitutional Proposition

---

## Purpose

Define the candidate
constitutional principle

that normative semantics
claimed to be reusable
across independent domains

shall remain independent
from the semantics
of any single domain.

This principle
does not require
a particular
Common Trust Architecture.

It governs
the validity
of cross-domain
normative claims.

---

## Constitutional Proposition

A normative abstraction
claimed to be
cross-domain

shall not derive
its meaning
from one
specific domain.

No Domain Runtime
shall silently redefine
a validly shared
cross-domain
normative semantic.

Cross-domain
normative authority

shall require evidence
that the abstraction
remains meaningful
outside
the domain
from which
it originated.

---

## Scope

CP-001 applies only
to normative semantics
that claim:

Cross-domain applicability.

Platform-level reuse.

Shared repository
normative meaning.

CP-001 does not require
every repository capability
to become cross-domain.

Domain-local semantics
shall remain
domain-local.

---

## Non-Goals

CP-001 shall not define:

Common Trust Architecture.

Trust Kernel.

Trust Profiles.

Evidence schemas.

Replay semantics.

Certification.

Attestation.

Runtime behavior.

Commercial product behavior.

Implementation technology.

Programming languages.

Frameworks.

Libraries.

Execution engines.

These concerns
belong to
lower normative layers.

---

## Domain Independence Requirement

A cross-domain
normative abstraction
shall remain meaningful
without requiring
the business semantics
of one specific domain.

A cross-domain abstraction
shall not require
knowledge of:

Retail.

Finance.

Healthcare.

Legal systems.

Manufacturing.

Scientific computing.

Artificial intelligence.

Computer vision.

Supply chain.

Robotics.

or any future
application domain

unless that knowledge
is explicitly
part of its
declared applicability
boundary.

---

## Domain Runtime Boundary

Domain Runtimes
may define:

Domain semantics.

Domain policy.

Domain decisions.

Domain actions.

Commercial behavior.

A Domain Runtime
may specialize
a shared normative
abstraction

only within
its declared
domain boundary.

It shall not
silently redefine
the shared
cross-domain meaning.

---

## Cross-Domain Claim Rule

A claim
of cross-domain
applicability
shall require
cross-domain evidence.

Success
within one domain
shall not establish
cross-domain validity.

Success
within multiple
similar domains
shall not automatically
establish
independence.

The evidence
shall be sufficient
to test
whether the abstraction
survives
materially different
domain semantics.

---

## Generalization Rule

Validated knowledge
may move
from a Domain Runtime

toward
shared repository
normative semantics

only when
the generalization
survives
cross-domain
refutation.

Domain success
shall not itself
create
cross-domain
normative authority.

---

## Falsifiability

CP-001
shall remain
a falsifiable
constitutional candidate.

The proposition
shall fail
or require narrowing
if evidence demonstrates
that:

Shared semantics
cannot remain meaningful
without domain-specific
knowledge.

Domain Runtimes
must repeatedly redefine
the shared semantics
to remain correct.

Cross-domain
generalization
creates greater
semantic ambiguity
than domain-local
specification.

The abstraction
cannot define
a stable
applicability boundary.

The claimed
cross-domain property
does not survive
independent domains.

---

## Relationship to Common Trust Architecture

Common Trust Architecture
is not required
by this principle.

CTA may emerge
as one architectural
response

if evidence demonstrates
that reusable
cross-domain
normative semantics
require
a common architecture.

CTA shall therefore
remain
an architectural
research candidate

until independently
justified.

CP-001 shall not
constitutionalize
an architectural
solution
before
the underlying
constitutional proposition
has survived
promotion.

---

## Relationship to RC-001

CP-001 remains
subordinate to

RC-001
Repository Constitution
Baseline 1.0.

CP-001 shall not
reinterpret:

Repository normative
authority.

Implementation independence.

Technology independence.

Domain boundary.

Constitutional minimality.

CP-001 specializes
the constitutional
question of

how cross-domain
normative claims
may acquire
repository-level authority.
