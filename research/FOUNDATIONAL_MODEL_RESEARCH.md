# Foundational Model Research

Version

0.1

Status

Research

---

## Purpose

This document records
the active research
into the Platform's
foundational abstraction.

This document
is intentionally
non-normative.

Its purpose
is not
to define
the final architecture.

Its purpose
is to challenge
candidate architectures
before promotion.

---

## Research Question

The current
foundational question is:

Is Trust
the fundamental
architectural abstraction,

or is Trust
an emergent property
of a more general
computational appraisal
process?

This question
remains open.

---

## Research Discipline

The research program
shall follow:

Evidence
over attachment.

Refutation
before adoption.

Explicit uncertainty
over false certainty.

Reproducibility
over anecdotal success.

Cross-domain validation
over premature
generalization.

---

## Initial Trust Hypothesis

An early candidate
Trust Kernel
contained:

Identity.

Integrity.

Artifact.

Execution.

Evidence.

Systematic reduction
demonstrated that
several of these
were not universally
irreducible.

The hypothesis
was therefore
rejected
as a universal
minimal model.

---

## Trust Model v2

A subsequent
candidate contained:

Subject.

Canonical Identity.

Integrity.

Evidence.

Further refutation
showed that
this model
could not adequately
represent:

Claims.

Evaluation context.

Appraisal criteria.

Provenance.

The distinction
between evidence support
and domain decisions.

The model
was therefore
rejected
as a universal
foundational model.

---

## Trust Model v3

The next candidate
introduced:

Claim.

Subject.

Context.

Evidence.

Provenance.

Policy.

Decision.

Refutation
demonstrated that:

Decision
was downstream
of trust appraisal.

Provenance
was not always
a primitive.

Context
could be represented
as evaluation input.

Subject
could be represented
within or referenced
by a Claim.

The model
was refined.

---

## Claim Appraisal Candidate

The current
minimal candidate
is:

Claim.

Evidence.

Appraisal Criteria.

Claim Assessment.

The working abstraction
is:

Appraise

Claim,

Evidence,

Appraisal Criteria

to produce

Claim Assessment.

---

## Epistemic Boundary

Claim Assessment
shall not be assumed
to establish
objective truth.

SUPPORTED
shall not mean
TRUE.

NOT_SUPPORTED
shall not mean
FALSE.

INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
shall remain distinct
from contradiction.

A Claim Assessment
describes only
the relationship
between:

A declared Claim.

Declared Evidence.

Declared Appraisal Criteria.

---

## Trust Boundary

Claim Assessment
shall remain distinct
from:

Trust interpretation.

Authorization.

Business approval.

Clinical authority.

Legal consent.

Financial authorization.

Operational action.

Domain decisions
remain downstream.

---

## Current Candidate Structure

Reality

remains outside
the Platform.

The Platform evaluates:

Claim

↓

Evidence
+
Appraisal Criteria

↓

Claim Assessment

↓

Domain Policy

↓

Domain Decision

The Platform
shall not claim
that Claim Assessment
is equivalent
to objective truth.

## Open Hypotheses

The following
questions remain open:

Is Claim
irreducible?

Is Evidence
irreducible?

Are Appraisal Criteria
a primitive
or merely
one category
of evaluation input?

Is Claim Assessment
a primitive
or simply
the outcome
of appraisal?

Is Computational Appraisal
more fundamental
than Trust?

Can a Common Trust Architecture
be derived
from a more general
Appraisal Architecture?

Does such generalization
add commercial value

become:

Trust Kernel.

## Research Exit Criteria

A foundational model
may advance
to architecture
only after:

Systematic refutation.

Internal consistency.

Cross-domain validation
where generality
is claimed.

Implementation independence.

Reproducible analysis.

Defined failure cases.

Defined applicability
boundaries.

Demonstrated
commercial relevance.
