# Candidate Architecture Knowledge Families

The surviving knowledge
can be reduced
into four candidate families.

---

## Family A — Scope and Authority Separation

Includes:

Cross-domain
generalization discipline.

Domain authority boundary.

Shared versus
domain semantics.

Reuse does not
create authority.

Implementation does not
create authority.

Shared terminology
does not prove
shared semantics.

Candidate principle:

Authority and semantic scope
shall remain explicit
and shall not expand
through implicit reuse,
interpretation,
or implementation behavior.

---

## Family B — Explicit Assumptions

Includes:

Material trust assumptions.

Epistemic premises.

Refutability.

Temporal assumptions.

Partial assumptions.

Distributed assumptions.

Property-specific assumptions.

Candidate principle:

Architectural claims
shall identify
material assumptions
that affect
their validity.

Those assumptions
shall remain
traceable
and refutable.

---

## Family C — Normative / Implementation Boundary

Includes:

Reference implementation
subordination.

Implementation substitution.

Conformance.

Normative ambiguity.

Evidence from implementation.

Candidate principle:

Implementation behavior
may provide evidence

but shall not silently
create,
resolve,
or redefine
normative semantics.

---

## Family D — Generalization Discipline

Includes:

Cross-domain evidence.

Scope-matched evidence.

Repeated override
as de-generalization signal.

Meaningful reuse.

Avoidance of
semantic emptiness.

Candidate principle:

A proposition
shall not be generalized
beyond the scope
supported by evidence.

Repeated exceptions,
overrides,
or specialization pressure

shall count
as evidence
against generalization.
