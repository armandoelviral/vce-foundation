Target

CP-002 Version 0.1 Draft

Title

Trust Model First

Refutation Type

Authority vs Epistemic Precedence

Status

Research

## Purpose

Determine whether
CP-002 Version 0.1

correctly distinguishes

epistemic precedence

from

normative authority.

The candidate asserts:

The Trust Model
shall define
the architecture.

Architecture
shall define
normative specifications.

The refutation asks
whether this direction
represents:

A valid reasoning
dependency.

A valid normative
authority hierarchy.

Both.

Or neither.

# RT-001 — Explicit Trust Assumptions

## Claim

Architectural reasoning
should begin
from explicit
trust assumptions.

## Attack

Can architecture
be meaningfully evaluated
without identifying:

Trusted actors.

Untrusted actors.

Trust boundaries.

Adversarial capabilities.

Failure assumptions.

Authority assumptions.

## Analysis

Not reliably.

Architecture may exist
without an explicit
Trust Model,

but its security
and trust properties
then depend upon
implicit assumptions.

Making those assumptions
explicit improves
refutability
and architectural reasoning.

## Result

SURVIVES
AS EPISTEMIC DISCIPLINE.

---

# RT-002 — Trust Model as Normative Authority

## Claim

The Trust Model
shall define
the architecture.

## Attack

Does explanatory
or design precedence
grant normative authority?

## Analysis

No.

A Trust Model
may constrain
architectural reasoning

without becoming
the source
of repository
normative authority.

Normative authority
is governed
by RC-001

and applicable
promotion mechanisms.

## Result

REFUTED
AS AUTHORITY CLAIM.

# RT-003 — Architecture Defines Specification

## Claim

Architecture
shall define
normative specifications.

## Attack

Can Architecture
be treated
as the authority
from which
Normative Specifications
derive?

## Analysis

Not categorically.

Architecture
may motivate,

constrain,

organize,

or be constrained by

Normative Specifications.

But authority
cannot be inferred
merely from
design dependency.

## Result

REFUTED
AS UNIVERSAL
AUTHORITY DIRECTION.

# RT-004 — Normative Specification to Contract

## Claim

Normative specifications
shall define
Executable Contracts.

## Attack

Is this necessarily
an authority relation?

## Analysis

Executable Contracts
may operationalize
normative requirements.

They shall not
create
higher normative authority.

The relationship
may be valid
as realization
or conformance,

but not as proof
of a complete
constitutional hierarchy.

## Result

SURVIVES
WITH NARROWING.

# RT-005 — Implementation Feedback

## Claim

The direction
shall never
be reversed.

## Attack

reveals:

An impossible invariant.

A hidden assumption.

A performance boundary.

A nondeterministic behavior.

A security defect.

May this evidence
cause reconsideration
of architecture
or specification?

## Analysis

Yes.

SL-001 explicitly permits
evidence-driven
backward transition
during normative maturation.

Implementation
shall not create
normative authority,

but implementation evidence
may refute
higher-level propositions.

## Result

ABSOLUTE
ONE-WAY DIRECTION
REFUTED.

## Attack

## Analysis

No.

Evidence flow
and authority flow
are different relations.

Lower-level evidence
may challenge
higher-level propositions

without acquiring
higher normative authority.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION
IDENTIFIED.

# Refutation Findings

CP-002 Version 0.1
contains a valid
epistemic insight

but conflates:

Epistemic precedence.

Normative authority.

Evidence flow.

Design dependency.

Lifecycle methodology.

Commercial evidence.

Repository evolution.

The original
one-direction hierarchy

Trust Model

↓

Architecture

↓

Normative Specification

↓

Executable Contract

↓

Implementation

cannot survive
as a universal
authority hierarchy.

# Surviving Distinctions

The following
relations shall remain
analytically distinct:

Normative Authority.

Epistemic Precedence.

Evidence Flow.

Design Dependency.

Implementation Realization.

against
a higher-level proposition

without obtaining
higher normative authority.

A concept
may precede another
during reasoning

without possessing
authority over it.
