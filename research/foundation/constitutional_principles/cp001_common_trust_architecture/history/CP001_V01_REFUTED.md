# Constitutional Principle

Identifier

CP-001

Title

Common Trust Architecture

Version

0.1

Status

Draft

---

## Purpose

Define the constitutional
requirement for a reusable
Common Trust Architecture
across independent
Domain Runtimes.

This principle is
a constitutional candidate.

It shall remain
Draft until validated
through independent
Domain Runtimes
and systematic
refutation.

---

## Principle

The repository
shall not implement
domain-specific
trust mechanisms
when those mechanisms
represent reusable
trust semantics.

The repository
shall pursue
a reusable
Common Trust Architecture.

Every conforming
Domain Runtime
shall inherit
common trust semantics
from the Platform.

Only domain semantics
may vary.

---

## Applicability Boundary

The Common Trust Architecture
shall only apply
when a computational
result requires
one or more
of the following:

Defensibility.

Reproducibility.

Attribution.

Verifiability.

Traceability.

The Common Trust Architecture
shall not be imposed
upon software
without meaningful
trust requirements.

Ordinary CRUD systems,

generic APIs,

ordinary microservices,

and unrelated
application infrastructure

shall not be required
to adopt
the Common Trust Architecture.

---

## Architectural Minimalism

The success
of the Common Trust Architecture
shall not be measured
by everything
it can contain.

Its success
shall be measured
by its ability
to remain:

Small.

Stable.

Domain-independent.

Implementation-independent.

Reusable.

The Common Trust Architecture
shall remain minimal
while Domain Runtimes
evolve around it.

Capabilities belonging
exclusively to
a Domain Runtime
shall not enter
the Common Trust Architecture.

---

## Architectural Role

The Common Trust Architecture
shall define
normative architectural
interfaces and semantics.

It shall not
implement
domain behavior.

It shall not
define
business decisions.

It shall not
define
domain-specific
policies.

It shall not
become
a software framework.

Domain behavior
shall remain
within
Domain Specifications
and Domain Runtimes.

---

## Framework Prohibition

The Common Trust Architecture
is an architecture.

It is not
a framework.

It shall not
require:

A programming language.

A class hierarchy.

A framework.

An SDK.

A library.

A database.

A cloud provider.

An execution engine.

A computer vision engine.

An AI model.

A specific
deployment technology.

---

## Domain Runtime Relationship

The Common Trust Architecture
shall enable
Domain Runtimes.

It shall not
replace them.

A Domain Runtime
shall define
its own
domain semantics.

A Domain Runtime
shall not
redefine
common trust semantics
that have been
validly promoted
into the Platform.

---

## Commercial Boundary

The Common Trust Architecture
shall not be
the commercial product.

The Common Trust Architecture
shall enable
commercial products.

Every Domain Runtime
shall maintain
its own
commercial narrative
based upon
the problem
it solves.

Commercial users
shall interact
with domain capabilities,

not with
internal architectural
layers.

---

## Research Constraint

The Common Trust Architecture
shall remain
Draft

until validated
through at least
three sufficiently
independent
Domain Runtime contexts.

Candidate validation
contexts include:

Knowledge Runtime.

Compute Runtime.

Visual Runtime.

No abstraction
shall become
constitutional
solely because
it appears elegant.

Evidence shall govern
architectural promotion.

---

## Evolution Rule

The Common Trust Architecture
may evolve
while it remains
Draft.

Evolution shall follow
evidence
over attachment.

A proposed abstraction
that fails
cross-domain validation
shall be removed,

refined,

or returned
to research.

Once validly frozen,

implementation technologies
shall evolve
around the architecture,

not redefine it.

---

## Candidate Invariants

Trust semantics
shall remain
reusable.

Domain semantics
shall remain
outside
the Common Trust Architecture.

Implementation technology
shall not define
the Common Trust Architecture.

Commercial products
shall remain
Domain Runtime concerns.

Architectural complexity
shall require
demonstrated necessity.

Evidence
shall precede
constitutional promotion.

---

# End of Principle
