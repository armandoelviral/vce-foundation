# C1-001 — Constitutional Necessity

## Hypothesis

A reusable
Common Trust Architecture

must exist
as a constitutional
repository principle.

---

## Attack

Could the same objective
be governed adequately
by:

Architecture Principles.

Architecture Specifications.

Domain Specifications.

or

the Common Trust Architecture
specification itself?

If yes,

CP-001 may be
architecturally important

without being
constitutional.

---

## Analysis

RC-001
already establishes
constitutional boundaries
for:

Repository normative
authority.

Implementation independence.

Technology independence.

Domain boundary.

Architecture subordination.

CP-001 therefore
cannot justify
constitutional authority
merely by repeating
those principles.

For CP-001
to remain constitutional,

it must express
a repository-level invariant
that would be lost
if CTA were governed
only as architecture.

---

## Result

NOT YET PROVEN.

---

## Finding

The existence
of a reusable CTA
is currently
an architectural hypothesis,

not yet
a demonstrated
constitutional necessity.

# C1-002 — Commonality Assumption

## Hypothesis

Trust semantics
exist
that are reusable
across independent
Domain Runtimes.

---

## Attack

What if
apparently common
trust semantics
turn out to be
domain-relative?

For example:

Identity.

Evidence.

Replay.

Certification.

Attribution.

may require
materially different
semantics
across domains.

---

## Analysis

CP-001 currently assumes
that reusable trust semantics
exist.

That assumption
has not yet been
fully validated
across sufficiently
independent domains.

---

## Result

REMAINS HYPOTHESIS.

---

## Falsification Condition

CP-001 shall fail
if repeated
cross-domain analysis
demonstrates
that no sufficiently
stable common semantics
exist

without importing
domain-specific meaning.

---

## Attack

Protocols.

Profiles.

Schemas.

Contracts.

requiring

Common Trust Architecture.

## Attack

in the repository.
