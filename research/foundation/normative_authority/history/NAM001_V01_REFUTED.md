# Normative Authority Model

Identifier

NAM-001

Version

0.1

Status

Draft

Model

Authority Graph Candidate

Authority

NONE.

---

## Purpose

Define a candidate model
for representing
normative authority
within the repository.

The model shall distinguish:

Authority.

Authority scope.

Authority source.

Subordination.

Classification.

Lifecycle state.

Implementation.

Evidence.

Historical identity.

No relationship
shall imply
normative authority
unless that authority
is explicitly established.

---

## Governing Context

NAM-001
is subordinate to
the currently authoritative
Repository Constitution.

NAM-001
does not modify
constitutional authority.

NAM-001
does not grant
authority to itself.

NAM-001
does not grant
authority to any
other repository artifact.

---

## Core Proposition

Repository normative authority
shall be represented
as explicit,
scoped relationships

rather than
as a universal
linear hierarchy.

Authority
shall not be inferred
from:

File location.

Directory depth.

Artifact age.

Implementation existence.

Dependency direction.

Execution order.

Reference count.

Historical popularity.

Naming convention.

Architectural importance.

Commercial importance.

---

## Authority Graph

Normative authority
shall be representable
as a graph.

Graph nodes
may represent
normative artifacts
or authority contexts.

Graph edges
may represent
explicit authority
relationships.

A graph edge
shall possess
defined semantics.

The existence
of an edge
shall not imply
universal superiority
of one artifact
over another.

---

## Repository Authority Context

Every normative
authority claim
shall exist
within an identified
Repository Authority Context.

Authority
outside its applicable
context
shall not be assumed.

Two artifacts
may possess
different authority
within different
contexts.

Context identity
shall remain explicit.

---

## Authority Scope

Authority
shall be scoped.

An authoritative artifact
shall possess authority
only over
the semantics
within its defined scope.

Authority in one scope
shall not automatically
create authority
in another scope.

Scope expansion
shall require
explicit normative action.

---

## Authority Source

Every authoritative
artifact
shall possess
a traceable
authority source.

Authority source
may include,
where applicable:

Bootstrap Authority.

Promotion Gate.

Authoritative predecessor.

Explicit authority transition.

Applicable governing artifact.

Authority shall not
be established
through inference.

---

## Direct Authority

Direct Authority
exists when
an artifact
possesses explicitly
established normative authority

within a defined
Repository Authority Context
and scope.

Direct Authority
shall remain
traceable.

---

## Derived Authority

Derived Authority
may exist
when an authoritative
governing artifact

explicitly permits
another artifact
to define
normative semantics

within a bounded scope.

Derived Authority
shall not exceed
the scope
permitted by
its authority source.

Derived Authority
shall not become
constitutional authority
unless explicitly
promoted as such.

---

## Delegated Authority

An authoritative artifact
may delegate
bounded normative authority

only when
such delegation
is permitted
by its governing
authority semantics.

Delegation
shall identify:

Delegator.

Delegate.

Scope.

Effective transition.

Applicable constraints.

Delegation
shall not imply
transfer of
all authority.

---

## Classification Authority

Classification Authority
is authority
to determine
the classification
of candidate artifacts

under an explicitly
defined classification model.

Classification Authority
does not automatically
grant normative authority
to the artifacts
being classified.

A classifier
may be authoritative

while every
current candidate
remains non-authoritative.

---

## Lifecycle Authority

Lifecycle Authority
governs
how candidate
normative artifacts

may progress
through defined
lifecycle states.

Lifecycle Authority
does not imply
semantic authorship
of every artifact
subject to that lifecycle.

Lifecycle state
and normative authority
shall remain distinct.

---

## Constitutional Authority

Constitutional Authority
is repository-level
normative authority

established
under the applicable
constitutional
authority mechanism.

Constitutional Authority
shall not be inferred
from a document
being called:

Constitutional.

Principle.

Foundation.

Core.

Permanent.

Frozen.

---

## Architecture Principle Authority

Architecture Principle Authority
shall exist
only after
an Architecture Principle
candidate

satisfies
the applicable
classification,
validation,
and promotion
requirements.

Classification
as an
Architecture Principle Candidate

shall not itself
create
Architecture Principle Authority.

---

## Specification Authority

A Normative Specification
may possess
bounded authority

within its explicitly
defined semantic scope.

Specification Authority
shall not supersede
higher applicable
normative authority.

Implementation conformance
shall not establish
Specification Authority.

---

## Executable Contract

An Executable Contract
may test
or enforce
claims derived from
applicable normative authority.

An Executable Contract
shall not become
the normative source
merely because
it is executable.

Passing tests
shall not by itself
create normative authority.

---

## Implementation Relationship

Implementations
may realize
normative semantics.

Implementation
shall not create
normative authority

merely through:

Existence.

Deployment.

Correct execution.

Performance.

Adoption.

Commercial success.

Historical use.

Implementations
shall remain
subordinate
to applicable
normative authority.

---

## Evidence Relationship

Evidence
may support,
challenge,
refute,
or establish
claims relevant
to normative evolution.

Evidence
shall not automatically
possess normative authority.

Normative authority
shall not automatically
invalidate evidence.

Where evidence
refutes
an authoritative assumption,

the applicable
revision process
shall remain available.

---

## Historical Authority

An artifact
may possess
historical authority

without possessing
current authority.

Historical authority
shall remain
traceable.

Loss of
current authority
shall not erase
historical identity.

Superseded,
replaced,
or invalidated
authority

shall remain
distinguishable
from artifacts
that were
never authoritative.

---

## Candidate Authority

A candidate artifact
may contain
normative propositions

without possessing
normative authority.

Candidate status
shall remain distinct
from authoritative status.

Repository presence
shall not constitute
promotion.

---

## No Authority

An artifact
may explicitly possess:

Authority

NONE.

This state
shall mean
that the artifact
possesses no
current normative authority.

Authority NONE
shall not prevent
the artifact
from possessing:

Research value.

Historical value.

Architectural value.

Evidence value.

Candidate value.

Implementation value.

---

## Authority Subordination

Where two
normative artifacts
operate within
overlapping scope,

a subordinate artifact
shall not contradict
applicable higher authority.

Subordination
shall be established
through explicit
authority semantics.

Directory structure
shall not establish
subordination.

---

## Authority Conflict

A normative conflict
exists when
two applicable
authority claims

within overlapping
scope

cannot simultaneously
be satisfied.

Conflict resolution
shall use
explicit authority
relationships

and applicable
Repository Authority Context.

Artifact recency
alone
shall not resolve
authority conflict.

---

## Authority Ambiguity

Authority ambiguity
exists when
the repository
cannot determine
from explicit evidence:

Whether authority exists.

What its scope is.

What its source is.

Whether it remains current.

What it supersedes.

What governs conflict.

Authority ambiguity
shall not be resolved
through assumption.

It shall require
explicit investigation
or normative action.

---

## Authority Transition

Authority
may transition
through an explicitly
defined normative event.

A transition
shall identify,
where applicable:

Prior authority state.

New authority state.

Effective version.

Authority source.

Promotion decision.

Supersession relationship.

Repository Authority Context.

Historical predecessor.

---

## Promotion

Promotion
is an explicit
authority transition.

Promotion
shall not be inferred
from:

Test success.

Merge.

Commit.

Tag.

Release.

Deployment.

Documentation status.

Implementation completion.

Promotion
shall require
the applicable
Promotion Gate
or authority mechanism.

---

## Supersession

Supersession
shall be explicit.

A newer artifact
shall not automatically
supersede
an older authoritative
artifact.

Supersession
shall identify
the affected
authority scope.

The superseded artifact
shall remain
historically traceable.

---

## Invalidation

Authority
may be invalidated
through an applicable
normative process.

Invalidation
shall not erase
historical authority.

An invalidated artifact
shall remain distinguishable
from an artifact
that was
never authoritative.

---

## Freeze

Freeze
shall not be treated
as a universal
authority layer.

Freeze
may constrain
change

within an explicitly
defined lifecycle,
release,
baseline,
or authority context.

Freeze
shall not create
normative authority
unless the governing
authority model
explicitly establishes
that effect.

---

## Orthogonal Models

Normative Authority
shall remain distinct
from:

Architecture layering.

Runtime processing.

Artifact processing.

Evidence processing.

Deployment topology.

Repository directory structure.

Lifecycle state.

These models
may reference
one another.

They shall not
be silently collapsed
into one hierarchy.

---

## Current Foundation Example

The current repository
demonstrates
that multiple
authority scopes
may coexist.

RC-001
possesses
constitutional
repository authority.

SL-001
possesses
bounded lifecycle
authority.

APC-001
possesses
bounded architecture-principle
classification authority.

These artifacts
shall not be interpreted
as three interchangeable
levels
of one universal
linear stack.

Their authority
shall be interpreted
according to
their respective
scope and
governing relationships.

classification model
